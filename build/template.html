How it works

Thousands of brain scans, averaged together

Scientists in the ENIGMA project collected MRI brain scans from research centers around the world. For each condition, they compared people who have it with people who don't, area by area.

Glowing means different

An area glows when it is reliably smaller or bigger, on average, in people with the condition. Brighter means a bigger difference.

It's an average

Every brain is different. Many people with a condition have brains that look completely typical in these areas.

Not a diagnosis

You can't tell if someone has a condition by looking at their brain this way. The differences only show up across large groups.

For scientists: methods and sources

Values are Cohen's d (case vs control, the toolbox's d_icv column) for cortical thickness in 68 Desikan–Killiany areas and volume in 16 subcortical structures. An area is shown as different when the difference passes FDR correction (q < .05; uncorrected p < .05 where only that was released: Parkinson's, and subcortical results for ADHD and depression) and |d| ≥ 0.2. "A little", "noticeably" and "much" mean |d| of 0.2–0.5, 0.5–0.8 and ≥ 0.8. Some older subcortical results average left and right. Epilepsy is all types combined. Data from the ENIGMA Toolbox (Larivière et al., 2021, Nature Methods).
